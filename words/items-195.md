# The owner's words behind the core: items 195 to 207

What these are and how they are kept: `words/README.md`.

195. **13 November 2026** (the core, in a chat with a session the owner had asked what it made of the core and the two
     games; not the design session). Told that the core was mechanically sound and not yet stable, and that its size
     cap, raised eight times in a day, measured growth more than it limited it, the owner asked where the core could
     still improve. Offered four improvements (a gate at the door, every new rule naming what went wrong without it and
     the check that holds it; releases, which a project of another owner takes in place of `main`; a starter kit written
     by a script; and the core measured as a game's features are), the owner agreed to all four.

196. **13 November 2026** (the core, the same chat as item 195). Told that the four improvements were merged and that
     cutting the first release was left to the owner, since the session could push no tag, the owner decided that the
     session does everything and the owner tags no commit, and asked for a trial on another platform and kind of
     project, and whether more of the rules could be grounded.

197. **13 November 2026** (the core, the same chat as item 195). Told that the starter kit set up Dependabot for another
     installer's packages while the core had no licence check for them yet, and that floor rule 10's list for what ships
     left out licences such packages meet, the owner asked whether there was good reason not to build that check in the
     core. Told there was none that held, the owner agreed to add all of the licences. The owner then asked what steps
     of the release were the owner's to take, and decided that the name on the account's profile stays.

198. **13 November 2026** (the core, the same chat as item 195). Asked whether a licence read by hand for a package that
     ships should need the owner's yes too, and whether the NuGet check should enforce from a project's first day and
     not after an advisory week, the owner agreed to both, the second yes read as a yes to the question as put, not to
     the session's own view to keep the week.

199. **13 November 2026** (the core and both games, in a chat with a session the owner had asked how the work went,
     whether it had questions and how the stand-in was doing). Told of the gaps in how the stand-in was asked and how
     its verdicts were recorded, and asked yes or no to three looks the stand-in was unsure of and whether to keep a
     reading taken against an answer of the owner's, the owner agreed to all, read as a yes to each look, to keeping the
     reading and to closing each gap named.

200. **13 November 2026** (the core, the same chat as item 199). Shown an orchestrator that called a stalled lane alive,
     and offered Opus in its seat, a script for the stall check, a wake set once and an end to a false alarm, the owner
     having said that Sonnet at high had stopped fully at least twice in the couple of days it held that seat, the owner
     agreed and asked which model to recommend; the answer, Opus at high with the script, was read as the owner's yes.

201. **13 November 2026** (the core, handed to its design session in a message of the owner's: the brief of an outside
     session that had audited the core at release-2 that day, which the owner had gone through point by point, the
     owner's word quoted under each section). On section 0, that nothing may lock another owner's project (what the
     brief said of that project here was not so, and is taken out: core archive 202), the owner asked for a guarantee
     that nothing of it would lock the owner's projects. On section 1, three ideas, the owner decided they be filed as
     issues, for later. On section 2, the personal-terms check reading every commit, the owner agreed if it is quick and
     adds little overhead, a private project paying nothing for it; told that it costs no billed minute and is on for
     every project, the owner decided that a missing secret `PERSONAL_TERMS` must not fail it. The owner agreed to
     sections 3 to 12, the one on another stack on the condition that branch protection and secret scanning lock no
     project's work where they are not set up, and to the two points the brief had only explained.

202. **14 November 2026** (the core, in a chat with the outside session of item 201, which the owner had asked to audit
     what that item built, at d76174c, and then to fix what it found). Told of four things left (a listed name that a
     machine's git writes into each commit failing every pull request, an early-dated commit read by no per-commit
     check, a report `core.yml` kept, and an upgrade test that started from one release alone), the owner agreed to make
     all of the fixes, big and small. Told that item 201 described a project of another owner from what the brief had
     said of it, the owner decided that it was incorrect and had it removed. Asked how the check should treat such a
     name, and having first asked whether it would block the owner's own account, the owner chose, of two answers
     offered, to block, with a failure the session mends itself.

203. **14 November 2026** (the core, the same chat as item 202). The owner asked why secondary security reviews are
     needed and, offered a cheaper option, what the session recommended. Told to keep two, with one cut, a change of
     comments and steps' names alone, proven so by a script, passing one security review, the owner agreed to that.

204. **14 November 2026** (the core, in the design chat). Told that a project could be played from its first stage on,
     unlike Amarna, which was built toward its finished form, the owner said that aiming Amarna at its end station had
     been a mistake, and that stages should feel playable. Asked whether the core should say that each stage of a build
     ends in a game someone can play from start to finish and enjoy, the owner agreed, naming Amarna's screen, which
     does not show what is going on, as the bigger problem.

205. **14 November 2026** (the core, in the design chat, the same as item 204). Told how a game's screen would be
     rebuilt so that a person sees at a glance what happened, what waits, what can be done and whether they are winning,
     the owner decided that the rules on playability apply equally to what a model seat sees in any game, so that all
     players play on terms as equal as possible.

206. **14 November 2026** (the core, in the design chat). Told why a pure sync of a project waited for a security review
     although the core's own pull request had passed both of its reviews, the owner recalled the rule that a review
     already running is waited for. Told that the session had just merged past a code review still running, the owner
     said that merging should ideally wait for the reviews to finish, without a bottleneck where a reviewer goes wrong
     or becomes unavailable.

207. **14 November 2026** (the core, in a chat with the session that fixed the bugs a review of the core's checks found,
     pull request #233). Told that the personal-terms, NuGet and comment-only checks now read what they had missed, so
     that a project which changed nothing of its own could fail on what it already held, the owner decided that what a
     project already held is reported, not failed, save a listed personal term, which fails, its failure saying how the
     session takes it out.
