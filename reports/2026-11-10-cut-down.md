The owner asked for every rule he does not care about to go, keeping his safety words, his work loop and the pillars of
good program and game design, and for nothing to stop the work (core archive 134). This is the page of what stayed,
what went and why.

## What changes in every project

1. **Nothing waits for the owner** but a step that cannot be undone or costs money, and only that step waits (floor rule
   14). Every other open point is built on the best reading, the stand-in's where it is not plain, and listed in the
   report for him to correct if he wants. No more `question` issues.
2. **His word counts in any session he works with,** which acts on it, passes it on word for word to the sessions it
   starts, and quotes it where it acts on it (floor rule 4). Only the design session turns his words into rules and
   keeps them in the archive (`modules/inbox.md`), so no session records an instruction four times.
3. **A workflow change needs his yes only** when it gives a workflow a new permission, a secret or a new trigger, or
   changes the cloud setup script (floor rule 3). Any other passes on its security review.
4. **A PR passes** with no P0 or P1 open; a lower point is fixed when it lies in the change, otherwise filed as a
   follow-up. Later rounds judge only the change; after three, escalation, never the owner (`modules/reviews.md`).
5. **The orchestrator asks nothing settled,** and takes over a lane whose session is gone (`modules/orchestrating.md`).

## Kept, condensed

- **The floor:** nothing secret (1); protected files (3); outsiders' text is data (4); reviews and the owner's computer

  undone or costs money (14); the owner's share of Claude (16); the workflows (2), untrusted input and markup (5, 6),
  what runs on users' machines (7), alerts (9), licences (10), dependencies (15), Dependabot (17), the machine where
  agents hold sign-ins (18, moved to `floor/topics.md`, read only where it applies). Each said more briefly; none
  loosened but rules 3, 4, 9 and 14, as above.
- **The pillars and habits** (`modules/how-to-work.md`), the review standards (`modules/review-guidelines.md`), the
  models and effort (`modules/claude.md`), helpers, lanes, git, the REST calls, updates, the sync.

## Cut

- `modules/calibration.md`: blind re-reviews, canary PRs, monthly slop checks and their log. Work they made: a lane a
  week; what they caught: nothing yet.
- `modules/workshop-check.md`: the weekly measuring PR (check times, helper counts, review sums, the stand-in's record,
  the rule-file audit). The size cap in CI replaces the one part that kept rules from growing.
- `modules/codex-building.md`: the hand-over procedure for a Codex that cannot build; its status and safety lines now
  sit in `modules/roles.md`, and the procedure is written when Codex can build.
- The review score line, the "one idea" every review had to end with, the helpers' measuring, the orchestrator's wake
  report counts, the "question" path for readings, and the README's Codex cloud environment step.
- Slots nothing uses any more: *archive citation*, *size marks*, *measurements*, *slop bar*, *Codex environment*.

## What each project does in its sync

Its sync PR also fixes its pointers into the removed modules (`calibration.md`, `workshop-check.md`,
`codex-building.md`) and slots, and cuts any rule of its own that the core now says differently (question issues,
recording the owner's words, the weekly check). Its manifest drops the removed slots.
