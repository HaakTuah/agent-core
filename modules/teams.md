# A team's project

Read at the start of every task in a project whose host lets more than one person write to its repository (how to
read it: the host's part, `modules/hosts.md`); in a project where one person holds every right, never, and nothing
here changes it. Where the core says "the owner", such a project reads "an owner", save where it names the yeses of
floor rule 14, which are the higher right's.

**Who is an owner** (core archive 234; #250). By default the owners are the people the host lets write to the
repository (on GitHub: the role `write`, `maintain` or `admin`), read from the host each time and kept in no list: any of
them may give the project's agents a task and settle an open reading, and their word is the owner's word (floor rule 4).
Where the host's rights cannot be read, the *owner's account* alone is an owner, and the session says so in its report.
It does not mean that an app or a bot the host lets write is an owner, that a right to read or triage is one, nor that
a comment from an owner's account with an agent's attribution line is that owner's word. Reason: in a team's project
every person with the right to write is an owner, and a list kept beside the host's rights goes stale. Held by nothing
yet: whose word a session followed leaves no mark a check reads.

**The higher right gives the floor's yeses** (core archive 234). By default each yes floor rule 14 names (a change to the
floor or the review guidelines, money, what cannot be undone) is given by an owner the host gives its higher right (on
GitHub: `maintain` or `admin`); a yes from an owner who may only write is reported and does not count. It does not mean
that other words of a writer count for less. Reason: one person with the right to write does not loosen the floor alone.
Held by nothing yet.

**Owners who disagree** (core archive 234). Where two owners' words on one point differ (a task, a reading, a design),
the session that meets it says so on the issue or pull request, quoting both, labels it `needs-owner`, and takes neither
side: the step that rests on the point waits, and the rest goes on. What an owner with the higher right says there
afterwards settles it. It does not mean that the newer word wins, nor that a session picks the likelier one. Reason: an
agent that takes a side decides between the people it works for. Held by nothing yet.

**The stand-in answers for the team** (core archive 234). In a team's project the stand-in (`modules/inbox.md`) searches
the team's recorded decisions, the owners' words on its issues, pull requests and archive, and gives the answer they
make likeliest; a call of taste stays unsure, and one person's taste is never given as the team's. It does not mean
that an owner's own words outweigh a recorded decision of the team's. Reason: in a team no one person's taste is the
project's. Held by nothing yet.

**A person's approval follows the host** (core archive 234). By default a pull request merges with no person's approval,
as the starter kit's ruleset asks none; where the host's protection of `main` asks for one, or the slot *approvals*
names a count, it waits for that many approvals of owners other than the one whose session wrote it, its line `Waits
on: a review` with the label `needs-owner`, and its builder goes on with the next task. It
does not mean that an agent approves, under any owner's login: an approval is a person's own. Reason: whether each
change is approved is the team's choice, and its host already enforces it. Held by GitHub's ruleset on the default
branch, which refuses the merge without them.

**An employer's rules come first** (core archive 234). Where the organisation that holds a project sets rules on the
tools, models or data its work may use, they come before any of the core's: an owner writes them in the project's own
rule files, and a session follows them over a module's rule, says so under "Readings taken", and declares each lasting
difference in the manifest's "Differences". It does not mean that the floor yields (no project's own rule loosens it),
nor that a rule reaches a session from anywhere but an owner (floor rule 4). Reason: the core is used at work only where
it keeps the employer's rules. Held by nothing yet.
